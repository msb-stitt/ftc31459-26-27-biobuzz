111 what’s happening: the classes your OpMode builds on¶

When the computer runs your program, it reads one line of code at a time. It moves words and numbers around in its memory. It is like moving sticky notes around on your desk.

In L110 you wrote two classes, L110FollowerOpMode and L110FollowerDriveTrain. Each one builds on classes you did not write. This page draws them as UML, as E067 and L093 did, a little at a time.

who wrote what¶

Your code is given a lot that you did not write. The diagrams put each class in a grey box that says who wrote it:

  • FTC Platform is the FTC software that runs on the robot. It runs your OpMode.

  • Pedro Pathing is the path follower’s code.

  • Our base classes are in base/. Our team wrote them for these lessons.

Your classes are outside the boxes.

is a¶

An arrow with a hollow triangle means “is a kind of”. The triangle points at the class built on. It is the word extends. L110FollowerOpMode extends CorbelsTeleOp, so an L110FollowerOpMode is a CorbelsTeleOp, which is a CorbelsOpMode, which is an OpMode.

A class gets everything the classes above it have. That is why gamepad1 and hardware work in your OpMode, though you never made them. gamepad1 comes from OpMode, and hardware from CorbelsOpMode. Each lesson’s OpMode since L020 has started with extends CorbelsTeleOp.

  • «interface» on Drivetrain marks a list of methods with no code in them. A dashed line with a hollow triangle means “is a kind of”, for an interface. It is the word implements. CorbelsDriveTrain implements Drivetrain, so it has to write each of those methods.

  • «abstract» on CorbelsDriveTrain marks a class that leaves a method for the class below it. It leaves mixArcadeToWheelSpeeds, and you wrote it in L110S030.

@Override sits above a method that a class above already names. OpMode names init() and loop() with no code, so each OpMode has to write them. CorbelsOpMode already has a start() and a stop(), and yours replace them.

Your init() calls initBefore() and initAfter(). Those are final in CorbelsOpMode: a class below can call them, but it cannot replace them.

has a¶

An arrow with a diamond means “holds”, as in E067. On an arrow from your OpMode, the word is the name of the field.

  • Your OpMode holds hardware and follower. CorbelsOpMode declares them, and your OpMode gets them.

  • You typed drivetrain and stickShaper.

  • The follower holds your drivetrain too, from initAfter(drivetrain). It holds it as a Drivetrain. It does not know your class, only that it is a kind of Drivetrain. So it can drive any drivetrain that is.

  • RobotHardware holds the robot’s motors. In the simulator, the same hardware holds simulated motors, so your code runs on both without a change.

Platform is not a class in the code. It stands for the FTC code that runs your OpMode.

what the platform calls¶

This sequence diagram starts with a stick figure: the driver. When the driver presses INIT, the platform calls init() once. Then it calls init_loop() over and over, until PLAY. A box marked loop holds calls that happen again and again.

Your code never calls init() or loop(). The platform does, when the Driver Station’s buttons are pressed. Each note names the diagram that shows what the call does.

INIT¶

  • initBefore() finds the robot’s motors, and sets hardware.

  • new makes your drivetrain, handed hardware.

  • initAfter(drivetrain) makes the follower, holding your drivetrain.

  • Until PLAY, each init_loop() asks the follower where the robot is. Your OpMode has no init_loop() of its own; it gets the one in CorbelsOpMode.

PLAY¶

  • start() hands driveFromTheSticks to the scheduler, once.

  • Each loop() ends with loopAfter(). That runs the scheduler, which calls your driveFromTheSticks(). Each stick goes through shaped(), and sticks(...) saves the wheel speeds.

  • Then loopAfter() updates the follower, and the follower calls your drivetrain’s drive. The wheels’ powers go to the motors.

STOP¶

stopAfter() tells the follower to stop, and then the drivetrain sends zero to each motor.

all of it at once¶

This diagram puts the “is a” and “holds” arrows together. A dashed arrow with an open head means “uses”, as in L093. Your OpMode calls Tracker and the Scheduler. Your drivetrain is made from RobotHardware.

No box here lists fields or methods. Each diagram on this page shows what it is about and leaves out the rest. A drawing of the whole program, with everything in it, would be too big to follow.
