# 131 lesson: check L113's numbers by measuring them

:::{figure} ../_figures/fig-l131-nothing-pushed.png

fig-l131-nothing-pushed.png

L113 worked out two numbers from the robot's parts and a tape measure: how many encoder ticks make
an inch, and the turn radius. Now the robot measures the same two numbers while you push it and
spin it by hand. Where a measured number differs from the worked-out one, the measured one goes in
the robot's own file.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L130 is done, and L113, so the robot's own file holds the ticks per inch and the turn radius
  worked out from its parts.
- The Pinpoint is mounted, as in L115, and its offsets set, as in L117, because this measurement
  believes it.
- AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop.
- The robot, the Driver Station, about 1.5 m of clear straight floor for the push, and room to spin
  the robot on the spot.
::::

## the steps

### L131S010: copy the three starters into mytry

This lesson has three files. Two are OpModes, one for each measurement. The third is the
drivetrain, which tells them what each wheel's encoder has counted.

**Copy.** In `lessons`, click `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode` once. Hold Shift and click
`L131TicksDriveTrain`, so all three are picked. Copy them, click `mytry` once, and paste. In the
**Copy** box, click **OK**. If Android Studio asks whether to add the files to Git, click **Add**.

Copy all three at once. Copied one at a time, the OpModes still point at the drivetrain in
`lessons`, and turn red.

**Check.**

- `mytry` holds `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode`, `L131MeasureTurnRadiusOpMode` and
  `L131TicksDriveTrain`, and none of them shows red.

### L131S020: switch both OpModes on

**Change.** In `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode`, delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that
names this step. Do the same in `L131MeasureTurnRadiusOpMode`.

**Check.**

- In both, under the step's comment, the next line reads
  `// 'public' so the robot can run this OpMode. 'public' allows access from any code.`

### L131S030: open a door to the encoders

Open `L131TicksDriveTrain`. A drivetrain sends four speeds out to the motors. `wheelTicks()` is
the same shape pointing the other way: it brings four numbers back.

**Change.** In `wheelTicks()`, find the comment that names this step. Under it, type:

```{code-block} java
:caption: mytry/L131TicksDriveTrain.java
:emphasize-lines: 4-7

        // When on L131S030, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l131.html#l131s030
        // for what to do here.
        ticks[FL] = hardware.frontLeft.getCurrentPosition();
        ticks[FR] = hardware.frontRight.getCurrentPosition();
        ticks[BL] = hardware.backLeft.getCurrentPosition();
        ticks[BR] = hardware.backRight.getCurrentPosition();
```

`getCurrentPosition()` is how many ticks a motor's encoder has counted since the robot started.
`FL`, `FR`, `BL` and `BR` name the slots, as in L113.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020, click **2D Field**, and open the `measure`
folder.

**Check.**

- `pushed_in` and `ticks` read 0.0.
- `measuredTicksPerInch` reads NaN, which means "not a number".

Nobody can push a robot in the simulator, so it stays at x 72 in and y 10.5 in. And 0.0 divided by
0.0 is not a number. The measuring is for the robot.

### L131S040: push the robot and measure an inch

An encoder counts small steps, called **ticks**, as its motor turns. Many ticks make one turn of
the wheel.

The robot keeps two accounts of the same trip. The Pinpoint says how far it went in inches, since
its own wheels measure the floor. The drive encoders say how many ticks they counted. One divided
by the other is the number.

**Robot.** Stop the simulator. Send the code, and run `L131 Measure ticks per inch` from the
Driver Station.

**Push.** Push the robot slowly straight forward, at least 36 in, keeping it pointing the same way.
Push slowly: a wheel that slips travels without turning, and the answer comes out short.

**Check.**

- The Driver Station shows `measuredTicksPerInch` with a number after it.

Do it three times. Three answers a tick or two apart are a measurement.

**Change.** Open the file of the robot `thisRobot` names, in the `pedro` folder, such as `Bot37523`.
Put the number in `ticksPerInch`, in place of the one L113S060 worked out. Send the code again and
run it once more to check.

Compare the two. On the Strafer, L113S060 worked out 41.8. A gap of more than a tick or two means
the wheel rolls as if it were a little bigger or smaller than the 104 mm on its page.

### L131S050: spin the robot and measure the radius

A wheel `r` inches from the middle travels `r` inches for every radian the robot turns. So the
radius is the wheel's inches divided by the radians. This OpMode turns ticks into inches with
`ticksPerInch`, so L131S040 comes first.

**Robot.** Run `L131 Measure turn radius` from the Driver Station.

**Spin.** Spin the robot on the spot by hand, at least two full turns, keeping it in about the same
place.

**Check.**

- The Driver Station shows a turn radius.
- It is close to the turn radius L113S070 worked out, A + B.

**Change.** Put the number in `turnRadiusInches` in the robot's own file, in place of L113S070's.

A gap here can mean the rollers do not push at quite 45°. L132 looks into why.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** None of the runs above needs one. Pushing a robot by hand is not something the
simulator does, so L131S040 and L131S050 are robot steps only.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l131_theTicksDoorReportsWhatEachEncoderCounted` fails, the test
puts 10, 20, 30 and 40 on the four encoders, so the numbers in the message name the wheel they came
from. Each of these was planted and run.

- `front left, front right, back left, back right: arrays first differed at element [0];
  expected:<10> but was:<0>`: L131S030 is not typed yet. Every measurement would say the robot
  never moved.
- `arrays first differed at element [1]; expected:<20> but was:<30>`: the front right and back
  left went into each other's slots. 30 is the back left's count, in the front right's place.

`getVelocity()` in place of `getCurrentPosition()` does not build. Android Studio underlines it
red, and the test run stops with:

```
error: incompatible types: possible lossy conversion from double to int
```

`getVelocity()` is how fast a wheel turns, as a decimal. `getCurrentPosition()` is the one that
counts.

With `@Disabled` left in, every test passes. The OpMode is missing from the Driver Station's list.

**If it didn't**, on the robot, and it says `Encoders counted 0 ticks` while the inches climb,
L131S030 is not on the robot you sent to.

**If it didn't**, and the turn radius is about half what the tape says, the robot was swung around
the room rather than spun on the spot. The Pinpoint counts the turning, and driving in a circle adds
turning the wheels never made.

**The wheels have to roll.** Both OpModes make the wheels coast in `start()` and put braking back in
`stop()`. It is given code, and the other two `l131_` tests watch it. They pass on the starter. Each
of these was planted in `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode` and run.

- `allowConfiguredBrakeMode()` added at the end of `measureTicksPerInch()`: the coast lasts one
  loop, and the test says `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode still coasts five loops in`. The wheels
  would brake while the student was still pushing.
- `allowConfiguredBrakeMode()` taken out of `stop()`: two messages,
  `L131MeasureTicksPerInchOpMode brakes again afterwards` and `braking is back although no loop
  ran`. The second is a run where STOP came before the first loop, which leaves the next OpMode
  coasting.

**What you just did.** Two worked-out numbers became measurements. L113 worked them out from
goBILDA's pages and a tape measure. No code could tell whether they were right, because code cannot
check a number it has no other way of knowing. Here the robot measured the same things with two
instruments that do not talk to each other, the Pinpoint and the drive encoders, and the
measurements were compared with the sums. Work a number out, then measure it before trusting it.
