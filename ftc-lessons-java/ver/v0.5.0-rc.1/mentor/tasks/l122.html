122 lesson: compare your localizers with the Pinpoint’s¶

Your localizers from L113 and L120 run beside the robot’s own. Yours from L113 reads the drive wheels. Yours from L120 reads the Pinpoint’s pods once a loop. The robot’s own reads the answer the Pinpoint worked out on its own chip. All three are published so they can be graphed against each other. None of them is the truth. A tape measure is.

Before you start.

  • L113 and L120 are done, so mytry holds L113EncoderLocalizer and L120PinpointLocalizer, and L117 is done, so the Pinpoint is on the robot and tuned.

  • AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop, and a gamepad is plugged into the laptop.

  • The robot on blocks, as in L050, the Driver Station, a tape measure, and about 3 m of clear floor for the last step.

the steps¶

L122S010: copy the starter into mytry¶

Copy. In lessons, click L122CompareLocalizersOpMode once and copy it. Click mytry once and paste. In the Copy Class box, click OK. If Android Studio asks whether to add the file to Git, click Add.

Check.

  • mytry holds L122CompareLocalizersOpMode, beside your L113EncoderLocalizer and L120PinpointLocalizer.

L122S020: switch your copy on¶

Change. In L122CompareLocalizersOpMode, delete the line @Disabled, under the comment that names this step.

Check.

  • Under the step’s comment, the next line reads // 'public' so the robot can run this OpMode. 'public' allows access from any code.

L122S030: run your localizers beside the Pinpoint’s¶

Change. In L122CompareLocalizersOpMode, find shadowLocalizers() and the comment that names this step. Under it, type the same three lines as in L113S030, and then the ones from L120S030:

        // When on L122S030, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l122.html#l122s030
        // for what to do here.
        HardwareWheelSource wheelSource = new HardwareWheelSource(hardware);
        L113EncoderLocalizer driveWheelEncoders = new L113EncoderLocalizer(wheelSource);
        shadowLocalizers.add("driveWheelEncoders", driveWheelEncoders);

        PinpointSource pinpointSource = HardwarePinpointSource.find(hardwareMap);
        L120PinpointLocalizer pinpointPods =
                new L120PinpointLocalizer(pinpointSource, Constants.localizerConfig);
        shadowLocalizers.add("pinpointPods", pinpointPods);

HardwareWheelSource, PinpointSource, HardwarePinpointSource and Constants turn red. Click each one and press Option+Enter, as in L113. L113EncoderLocalizer and L120PinpointLocalizer do not turn red. They are your own, and they sit in mytry too.

Simulator. In the Terminal, run:

./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L122CompareLocalizersOpMode --pad"

AdvantageScope. Connect to the simulator as in L020. Open the Localizer folder, then driveWheelEncoders, pinpointPods and pinPoint inside it.

Check.

  • Push the left stick forward. driveWheelEncoders/y_in and pinpointPods/y_in climb with pinPoint/y_in, and the three stay within a tenth of an inch.

L122S040: see how often the loop runs¶

Your localizers work out where the robot is once each time loop() runs. The Pinpoint’s chip works it out on its own, about 1500 times a second. Between two of your loops, the robot moves a little, and your localizer takes that move as one step.

AdvantageScope. Leave the simulator running. Open the loop folder and graph period_ms. It is how long the last loop took. It is measured as the OpMode runs.

Check.

  • period_ms is a few milliseconds or more, and it is not the same from one loop to the next.

The Pinpoint’s chip takes about 0.7 ms a step. Write down what period_ms reads in the simulator, to compare with the robot in the next step.

L122S050: drive it, then measure it¶

Robot. Stop the simulator. Send the code, run L122 Compare Localizers from the Driver Station, and watch it in AdvantageScope.

Check.

  • Drive about 80 in straight ahead and stop. The three y_in numbers moved, and they do not quite agree.

  • Measure how far the robot really went with the tape measure.

  • Read loop/period_ms on the robot, and compare it with what you wrote down in L122S040.

Then put the robot on the blocks and push the left stick forward. The wheels spin, and the robot does not go anywhere. driveWheelEncoders walks off across the field. pinpointPods and pinPoint stay put, because the pods are off the floor too.

No gamepad. --args="mytry.L122CompareLocalizersOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.5" drives forward.

If it didn’t, and driveWheelEncoders or pinpointPods is missing from the Localizer folder in L122S030, its three lines are not in shadowLocalizers(). LessonsTest.l122_yourLocalizersAreLogged fails with the shadow localizer is logged: for driveWheelEncoders, or L120's localizer is logged: for pinpointPods, and the list of names that were published.

What you just did. Your localizer from L113 believes the drive wheels, and a wheel that spins without carrying the robot moves its estimate. The Pinpoint’s pods only measure and never push, so they slip less. That is the case for having them. Your localizer from L120 and the Pinpoint’s read the same pods. They differ in how often they do the math: once a loop, or about 1500 times a second.
