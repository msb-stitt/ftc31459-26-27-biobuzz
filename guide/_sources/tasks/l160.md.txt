## The steps

### L160S010: copy the starter into mytry

**Copy.** In `lessons`, click `L160HeadingHoldOpMode` once and copy it. Click `mytry` once and
paste. In the **Copy Class** box, click **OK**. If Android Studio asks whether to add the file to
Git, click **Add**.

**Check.** `mytry` holds `L160HeadingHoldOpMode`, and the new file is open.

### L160S020: switch your copy on

**Change.** Delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that names this step.

**Check.** Under the step's comment, the next line reads
`// public: the robot only runs an OpMode that is public.`

### L160S030: watch the nose creep

:::{figure} ../_static/figures/fig-l160-creep.png
:name: fig-l160-creep

AdvantageScope's 2D Field with the robot near the bottom of the field, right of the middle, turned so its arrow points up and to the right, and the list showing `arcade/turn` at -0.040 and `pinPoint/heading_deg` at 30.070.
:::

The starter is L150's finished OpMode. This run pretends the turn stick rests a little off centre,
the way a worn stick does.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L160HeadingHoldOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.3 --right_stick_x=0.2"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020, click **2D Field**, and open the `arcade`
folder, then `Localizer` and `pinPoint`.

**Check.** `turn` reads -0.040, which is small. The robot drives to the right, and it turns as it
goes. `heading_deg` started at 90 and keeps dropping.

### L160S040: hold the heading

`HeadingHold` is written for you, in `base`. While the turn stick is pushed, it hands the stick
straight back and the driver steers. On the loop the stick is let go, it remembers the heading.
After that it hands back the turn that steers the robot back to that heading.

Its own deadband is 0.05, so a stick that is nearly centred counts as let go. `turn` at -0.040 is
inside it.

**Change.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. Find the line
`CorbelsMecanum drivetrain;` near the top of the class. Under it, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 2

    CorbelsMecanum drivetrain;
    HeadingHold hold = new HeadingHold(Constants.foresightConfig.headingFeedback.get());
```

`Constants.foresightConfig` holds the follower's own settings. `headingFeedback` is the part that
steers the heading in autonomous. Tune the follower, and this hold gets the same tuning.

`HeadingHold` and `Constants` turn red. Click each one and press Option+Enter, as in L120. The
popup for `Constants` names `org.firstinspires.ftc.teamcode.pedro.Constants`.

**Change.** In `loop()`, find the comment that names this step. Under it, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 6-7

        double turn = squared(deadband(-rightStickX, band));
        Tracker.publish("arcade/turn", turn);
        // When on L160S040, see
        // https://msb-stitt.github.io/ftc31459-26-27-biobuzz/guide/tasks/l160.html#l160s040
        // for what to do here.
        turn = hold.turn(follower, turn);
        Tracker.publish("heading/holding", hold.target() != null);
```

The first new line swaps the stick's turn for the hold's. Both `sticks` calls below use `turn`, so
both drive with it. Android Studio now underlines `turn` everywhere. That marks a variable that
changes after it is made. It is not an error.

**Simulator.** Run the same command again, and connect AdvantageScope. Open the `heading` folder
too.

**Check.** `holding` is green. The robot drives to the right, its nose still pointing up the field,
and `heading_deg` stays at 90.

### L160S050: drive it and then push it

**Send.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. Send the code to the robot, as in
L050. On the Driver Station, pick `L160 Heading Hold` from the `Lessons` group. Press INIT, then
PLAY.

**Check.** Drive a long diagonal with the left stick, and do not touch the turn stick. The nose
stays where it was. Then take hold of the robot and twist it a little. It turns back on its own.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** The two runs above need none. With `--pad`, a driver can turn, let go, and watch
the hold take over; that has not been performed.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l160_theRobotHoldsItsHeadingWhenTheStickIsReleased` fails with
`it steers back`, nothing listens to the hold. Either the line is missing, or it reads
`hold.turn(follower, turn);` without `turn =` in front, so its answer is thrown away.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with
`while steering, the stick wins expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`, the hold got `rightStickX` instead
of `turn`. That skips the minus sign that makes a push to the right turn clockwise.

**If it didn't**, and on the floor the robot fights back and overshoots, that is the follower's
tuning, not this lesson. L100 tunes it. If the nose drifts slowly anyway, the hold is working and
the follower's heading is drifting with it. Both are from the old `l13` page, and neither has been
seen on a robot.

The simulator turns the robot only when told to, so nothing there pushes it off its heading. The
creep in L160S030 is a stick resting off centre, not a bump.

**What you just did.** Three things want to turn the robot, and they take turns in a fixed order.
The driver's stick beats the hold. The hold beats doing nothing. The person with their hands on
the gamepad wins, and everything else fills in when they let go.

**Where next.** [L170](l170.md) gives a button a pose, and the robot drives there by itself while
the driver watches.
::::
