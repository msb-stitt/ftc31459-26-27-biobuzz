# L080: drive like an arcade game

:::{admonition} fig-l080-scaled
:class: pencil
AdvantageScope's Line Graph with `NT:/power/left` at 1 and `NT:/power/right` at 0.6 on
**Left Axis**, and the list showing `arcade/forward` at 1 and `arcade/turn` at -0.250.

Drive with one stick for going and one for turning: arcade drive. When the sticks ask for more power
than a motor has, scale every wheel down together, so the robot still drives the curve the driver
asked for.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L070 is done, so `mytry` holds `L070ButtonsOpMode`.
- AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop, and a gamepad is plugged into the laptop.
- The robot, the Driver Station, and about 3 m of clear floor for the last step.

Step L080S060 is written from the code and the old `l4` page. It has not been performed on a robot
yet.
::::

## The steps

### L080S010: copy the starter into mytry

**Copy.** In `lessons`, click `L080ArcadeOpMode` once and copy it. Click `mytry` once and paste.
In the **Copy Class** box, click **OK**. If Android Studio asks whether to add the file to Git,
click **Add**.

**Check.** `mytry` holds `L080ArcadeOpMode`, and the new file is open.

### L080S020: switch your copy on

**Change.** Delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that names this step.

**Check.** Under the step's comment, the next line reads
`// 'public' so the robot can run this OpMode. 'public' allows access from any code.`

### L080S030: forward and turn

Tank drive asks the driver to think in left wheels and right wheels. Arcade drive asks for forward
and turn instead, which is how a driver already thinks. The code works out the two sides.

The starter has the sticks, the buttons, `deadband` and `squared` from the lessons before. It does
not drive the wheels yet.

**Change.** In `loop()`, find the comment that names this step. Under it, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-8

        // When on L080S030, see
        // https://msb-stitt.github.io/ftc31459-26-27-biobuzz/guide/tasks/l080.html#l080s030
        // for what to do here.
        double band = 0.05;
        double forward = squared(deadband(-leftStickY, band));
        Tracker.publish("arcade/forward", forward);
        double turn = squared(deadband(-rightStickX, band));
        Tracker.publish("arcade/turn", turn);
```

`forward` comes from the left stick, up and down. `turn` comes from the right stick, sideways. Both
get a minus sign. For `forward`, that makes pushing away from you positive, as in L030. For `turn`,
it makes a turn to the left positive. That is the way robots measure turning: left, or
counter-clockwise, is positive.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L080ArcadeOpMode --pad"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020, and open the `arcade` folder.

**Check.** Push the left stick halfway forward, and `forward` reads 0.25. Push the right stick
halfway right, and `turn` reads -0.25. The robot does not move yet.

:::{admonition} fig-l080-forward-turn
:class: pencil
AdvantageScope's list with the `arcade` folder open, showing `forward` at 0.250 and `turn` at
-0.250, beside `stick/leftY` at -0.500 and `stick/rightX` at 0.500.

### L080S040: mix them into two sides

To turn right, the left side goes faster and the right side goes slower. A turn to the right is
below 0, so taking `turn` away from the left side speeds it up, and adding it to the right side
slows that side down.

**Change.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. Find the comment that names this
step. Under it, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-12

        // When on L080S040, see
        // https://msb-stitt.github.io/ftc31459-26-27-biobuzz/guide/tasks/l080.html#l080s040
        // for what to do here.
        double leftPower = forward - turn;
        double rightPower = forward + turn;
        Tracker.publish("power/left", leftPower);
        Tracker.publish("power/right", rightPower);

        hardware.frontLeft.setPower(leftPower);
        hardware.backLeft.setPower(leftPower);
        hardware.frontRight.setPower(rightPower);
        hardware.backRight.setPower(rightPower);
```

**Simulator.** Run the same command again, and connect AdvantageScope. Click **2D Field**. The
robot from L040 is still in **Poses**.

**Check.** Push the left stick forward, and the robot drives forward. Push the right stick right,
and it turns right. Push both, and it drives a curve.

Now push the left stick all the way forward and the right stick halfway right. Click
**Line Graph**, and drag `left` and `right` from the `power` folder into **Left Axis**.
`power/left` reads 1.25.

:::{admonition} fig-l080-too-much
:class: pencil
AdvantageScope's Line Graph with `NT:/power/left` at 1.25 and `NT:/power/right` at 0.75 on
**Left Axis**, and the list showing `arcade/forward` at 1 and `arcade/turn` at -0.250.
:::

A motor's power only goes from -1 to 1. On the robot, the Control Hub cuts 1.25 down to 1 and
leaves 0.75 alone. The left side loses some of what it asked for and the right side loses none, so
the robot drives a wider curve than the sticks asked for. The simulator does not cut it, so the
curve there looks right. The number shows the problem.

### L080S050: scale, don't cut

The fix is to divide both sides by the bigger one. 1.25 and 0.75 become 1 and 0.6. The robot goes
a little slower, and the curve stays the one the driver asked for.

**Change.** Press Ctrl+C to stop the simulator. Go back to the two lines from L080S040 that make
`leftPower` and `rightPower`. Under them, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 3-5

        double leftPower = forward - turn;
        double rightPower = forward + turn;
        double biggest = Math.max(1, Math.max(Math.abs(leftPower), Math.abs(rightPower)));
        leftPower = leftPower / biggest;
        rightPower = rightPower / biggest;
```

`Math.max` gives back the bigger of two numbers. The inner one finds the bigger power, with its
minus sign taken off. The outer one makes sure `biggest` is never less than 1. Without it, a gentle
0.25 would be divided by 0.25 and become full power.

The last two lines have no `double` in front. `leftPower` already exists, so they change it rather
than making a new one.

**Simulator.** Run the same command again, and connect AdvantageScope.

**Check.** Push the left stick all the way forward and the right stick halfway right. `power/left`
reads 1, and `power/right` reads 0.6. Push the left stick halfway forward alone, and both read
0.25, the same as before this step.

### L080S060: drive it on the robot

**Send.** Send the code to the robot, as in L050. On the Driver Station, pick `L080 Arcade` from
the `Lessons` group. Press INIT, then PLAY.

**Check.** Push the left stick halfway forward and the right stick a quarter right, and the robot
drives a circle to the right. Push both sticks twice as far, and it drives a circle the same size,
only faster.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** `--args="mytry.L080ArcadeOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.5 --right_stick_x=0.5"` holds
the sticks for the first picture. `--left_stick_y=-1 --right_stick_x=0.5` holds them for the other
two, before and after L080S050. The simulator has no walls, so a robot left running drives off the
field.

**If it didn't**, and the robot turns left when the right stick is pushed right, the minus sign is
missing from `-rightStickX`, or `-` and `+` are swapped in the two mix lines.

**If it didn't**, and the robot goes full speed with a gentle push, `biggest` starts below 1. Check
the `1` in the outer `Math.max`.

**What you just did.** Arcade drive mixes two things the driver wants into two things the wheels
do. When the mix asks for more than a motor has, dividing keeps the shape of the request and gives
up some speed. Cutting at 1 keeps the speed and changes the shape. The Control Hub cuts; it is the
code's job to scale first.
