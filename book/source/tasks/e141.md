# 141 what's happening: arrows as two numbers

Here you learn to write an arrow as two numbers, wherever it starts, to make it longer or
shorter, and to add two arrows. In L140, the stick's push and the robot's drive are arrows like
these.

This page and [E142](e142.md) are about the two lines you typed in [L140](l140.md):

```java
double robotForwardXSpeed = fieldXSpeed * Math.cos(heading) + fieldYSpeed * Math.sin(heading);
double robotStrafeYSpeed = -fieldXSpeed * Math.sin(heading) + fieldYSpeed * Math.cos(heading);
```

Each line is a sum of two pieces. To see why the pieces add up, we draw speeds as arrows. This
page shows how an arrow becomes two numbers.

An arrow has a length and a direction. We write it as two numbers, (x, y): how far it goes along
x, then how far along y. The arrow (3, 2) goes 3 along x and 2 along y. Those two moves are the
two short sides of a triangle, and the arrow is the long side.

:::{admonition} fig-e141-notation
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-e141-notation`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

An arrow does not have to start at (0, 0). Its two numbers are its tip minus its tail. From
(1, 2) to (4, 4) is (4 - 1, 4 - 2) = (3, 2), the same arrow as before. An arrow that goes left or
down has a number below 0.

:::{admonition} fig-e141-offset
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-e141-offset`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

To make an arrow twice as long, pointing the same way, multiply both numbers by 2:
2 × (3, 2) = (6, 4). Multiply by 0.5 and it is half as long.

:::{admonition} fig-e141-scale
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-e141-scale`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

This works for an arrow pointing any way, from any start. Multiply both numbers, even one
below 0, and the arrow keeps its direction and changes its length.

:::{admonition} fig-e141-scale-offset
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-e141-scale-offset`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

To add two arrows, put the second one's tail on the first one's tip. The sum runs from the first
tail to the last tip. Or add the numbers: (3, 1) + (1, 2) = (3 + 1, 1 + 2) = (4, 3). Both ways
give the same arrow.

:::{admonition} fig-e141-add
:class: pencil

:::

robot's own forward and strafe.
