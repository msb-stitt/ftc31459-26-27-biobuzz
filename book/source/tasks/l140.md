# 140 lesson: make the sticks mean the field

:::{admonition} fig-l140-spin-and-slide
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-l140-spin-and-slide`, a screenshot.
:::

Push the stick away from you and the robot goes away from you. It does that whichever way the robot
happens to be facing, so a driver never has to think about which end of the robot is the front.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L135 is done.
- AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop, and a gamepad is plugged into the laptop.
- The robot, the Driver Station, and about 2 m by 2 m of clear floor for the last step.

**Where the driver stands.** "Away from the driver" is the field's +x from the Red Alliance Area,
and -x from the Blue. From the Red Alliance Area, the robot starts at heading 90°, facing +y, so
at the start a push away from the driver slides it to its right.
::::

## the steps

### L140S010: copy the two starters into mytry

This lesson has two files. `L140FieldRelativeOpMode` is the teleop. `L140AutoOpMode` is L135's
auto with a choice to do nothing, and it hands the teleop the alliance. Both use L135's menu.

**Copy.** In `lessons`, copy `L140FieldRelativeOpMode` and `L140AutoOpMode` into `mytry`, one at a
time.

**Check.**

- `mytry` holds `L140FieldRelativeOpMode` and `L140AutoOpMode`.

### L140S020: switch your copies on

**Change.** In each copy, delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that names this step.

**Check.**

- In each, under the step's comment, the next line reads
  `// 'public' so the robot can run this OpMode. 'public' allows access from any code.`

### L140S030: drive and turn at once, the L110 way

The starter drives the way L110 did. Forward on the stick is forward for the robot.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L140FieldRelativeOpMode --pad"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020, click **2D Field**, and open the
`Localizer` folder, then `pinPoint`.

**Check.**

- Push the left stick a little forward and the right stick left, and hold both.
- The robot drives in a small circle, and `x_in` and `y_in` stay close to where they started.
- It goes where its nose points, and its nose keeps turning.

### L140S040: turn the stick backwards through the heading

:::{admonition} fig-field-relative
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-field-relative`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

You drive from the Red Alliance Area, as in the picture. From now on, `fieldXSpeed` means a speed
along the field's +x. It could be away from you or toward you, depending on which Alliance Area
you are in. `fieldYSpeed` means to your left, along the field's +y, when you are in the Red
Alliance Area. `fieldYSpeed` means to your right, along the field's +y, when you are in the Blue
Alliance Area. On AdvantageScope's 2D Field, +x runs to the right and +y runs up.

The wheels only know the robot's own forward and sideways. L120 turned a step on the robot into a
step on the field, through the heading. This turns the other way, from the field to the robot, so
the signs on `sin` swap.

**Change.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. In `driveFromTheSticks()`, find the
comment that names this step. Delete the line `drivetrain.sticks(fieldXSpeed, fieldYSpeed,
turnCcwSpeed);` under it, and type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-11

        // When on L140S040, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l140.html#l140s040
        // for what to do here.
        double heading_rad = follower.pose().heading();
        double sinHeading = Math.sin(heading_rad);
        double cosHeading = Math.cos(heading_rad);
        double robotForwardXSpeed = fieldXSpeed * cosHeading + fieldYSpeed * sinHeading;
        double robotStrafeYSpeed = -fieldXSpeed * sinHeading + fieldYSpeed * cosHeading;
        Tracker.publish("drivetrain/robotForwardXSpeed", robotForwardXSpeed);
        Tracker.publish("drivetrain/robotStrafeYSpeed", robotStrafeYSpeed);
        drivetrain.sticks(robotForwardXSpeed, robotStrafeYSpeed, turnCcwSpeed);
```

The follower knows which way the robot faces. The drivetrain does not. So the OpMode asks the
follower, and does the turn itself.

**Simulator.** Run the same command again, and connect AdvantageScope.

**Check.**

- Hold the same two sticks. The robot spins, and at the same time it travels to the right on the 2D
  Field, along +x.
- `x_in` climbs while `heading_deg` goes around and around.

### L140S050: turn the sticks round for the Blue Alliance Area

:::{admonition} fig-l140-field-blue
:class: pencil
Stand-in for `fig-l140-field-blue`, made by `book/tools/draw.py`.
:::

Find `fieldXSpeed = -leftStickY;` and `fieldYSpeed = -leftStickX;`, above the comment that names
this step. They are right for the Red Alliance Area. From the Blue Alliance Area, away from you is
the field's -x. Your left is -y. A push away makes `leftStickY` negative. So for blue,
`fieldXSpeed` is `leftStickY` with no minus. The same goes for `fieldYSpeed`.

The teleop has a menu too, with an `Alliance` row and a `Start` row.

**Change.** Stop the simulator. In `driveFromTheSticks()`, under the comment that names this step,
type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-8

        // When on L140S050, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l140.html#l140s050
        // for what to do here.
        String alliance = menu.chosen("Alliance");
        if (alliance.equals("Blue")) {
            fieldXSpeed = leftStickY;
            fieldYSpeed = leftStickX;
        }
```

**Simulator.** Run it with Blue chosen and the left stick pushed away:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L140FieldRelativeOpMode --init --dpad_right=true --a=true --left_stick_y=-0.4"
```

**Check.**

- The Terminal's last line reads `B-A`: Blue, and the start where the auto ended.
- Press Enter. The robot drives to the left on the 2D Field, along -x, and `x_in` falls.

### L140S060: drive it facing you

**Robot.** Stop the simulator. Send the code, and run `L140 Field Relative` from the Driver
Station. Stand off the robot's left side, so that away from you is the field's +x.

**Check.**

- Push the right stick until the robot's nose points at you.
- Then push the left stick away from you. The robot drives away from you while it faces you.
- Turn it a quarter turn and try again, and the stick still means the same way on the field.

### L140S070: hand the alliance from the auto to the teleop

At a match, the drive team chooses the alliance once, in the auto. `OpModeStorage` holds values
that last from one OpMode to the next, such as where the auto ended. At the top of
`L140AutoOpMode`, `preselectTeleOp = "L140 Field Relative"` has the Driver Station choose the teleop
when the auto ends.

**Change.** In `L140AutoOpMode`'s `start()`, under the comment that names this step, type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4

        // When on L140S070, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l140.html#l140s070
        // for what to do here.
        OpModeStorage.alliance = alliance;
```

`OpModeStorage` turns red. Click it and press Option+Enter.

**Robot.** Send the code. Run `L140 Auto` from the autonomous list. During INIT, choose `Blue` and
leave the auto at `N-Nothing`, so the last line reads `B-N`. Press PLAY, and let the auto run until
it stops on its own, after 30 seconds.

**Check.**

- The Driver Station now has `L140 Field Relative` chosen.
- Press INIT. Its menu's last line reads `B-A`.
- Stand off the robot's left side and push the stick away from you. The robot drives away from
  you.

**Robot.** Stop the teleop, and run it again with no auto first. During INIT, press the D-pad's
down, then right, then A, so the last line reads `B-W`. Connect AdvantageScope to 192.168.43.1, as
the Driver Station says.

**Check.**

- At PLAY, the robot is at the middle of the blue wall: `x_in` 72 and `y_in` 133.5.

**What's happening.** [E141](e141.md) and [E142](e142.md) show why your two lines work.
[E143](e143.md) shows the field from each Alliance Area.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** `--args="mytry.L140FieldRelativeOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.4 --right_stick_x=-0.7"`
holds both sticks, for the circle in L140S030 and the picture at the top. Before L140S040, the run
was watched at `x_in` 70.812 and `y_in` 7.230 after about 8 s.

After L140S040, the robot drifts toward +y as it goes: one run went from x 72 in and y 10.5 in
to x 179 in and y 43.5 in, about 17 degrees off a straight line. The
simulator has no walls, so a run left going drives off the field.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l140_fieldRelativeIgnoresWhichWayTheRobotFaces` fails with
`same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<1.0>`, the line still reads
`drivetrain.sticks(fieldXSpeed, fieldYSpeed, turnCcwSpeed);`.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with `expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`, the turn runs the wrong way.
The minus sign is missing from `-fieldXSpeed`, or both signs on `sinHeading` are the other way
around.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with
`same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<-0.3338674754196965>`, the
heading went in as degrees. `Math.cos` wants radians.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l140_fromTheBlueAllianceAreaTheStickMeansTheOtherWay` fails
with `away from the blue drivers is -x, behind a robot facing +x expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`,
the `if` from L140S050 is missing, or `"Blue"` is spelled differently.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with
`their left is -y, to the right of a robot facing +x expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`,
`fieldYSpeed` still has its minus inside the `if`.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l140_theAutoHandsItsAllianceToTheTeleOp` fails with
`the alliance handed to the teleop expected:<[Blue]> but was:<[Red]>`,
`OpModeStorage.alliance = alliance;` is missing from the auto's `start()`.

The Driver Station chooses the teleop only when the auto ends on its own or its 30 seconds run
out; pressing STOP leaves it unchosen. The drive team still presses INIT and PLAY for it, and its
menu works during INIT, as the auto's does.

**What you just did.** Field relative is one turn of two numbers and nothing else. The same
`sticks`, the same mix, the same scaling. All that changed is which pair of numbers goes in.

It also depends on the follower knowing which way the robot faces. If the heading drifts 20
degrees, the sticks are 20 degrees crooked. This is the first lesson where a mistake somewhere
else in the robot shows up in the driver's hands.

**Where next.** [L150](l150.md) puts the robot's own directions back on a bumper, so a driver can
have both.
::::
