# L100: let AutoTune tune the robot

The robot measures itself. It finds which way each motor turns and where the pods sit. It finds
how fast it can drive and how hard it can brake. Pedro Pathing's AutoTune does the measuring. You
paste what it finds into `Constants.java`.

Do this after L115, which mounts the Pinpoint this lesson tunes.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L090 is done, and L050's motor directions are set.
- The Pinpoint and its two pods are mounted and plugged in, as L115 says. L115 comes later in the
  book, but L100S030 and L100S040 need the pods.
- The robot, the Driver Station, a laptop that can join the robot's Wi-Fi, and blocks to lift the
  robot's wheels off the floor.
- Clear floor for L100S040: at least 48 in ahead of the robot, 48 in to its left and 36 in to its
  right, plus a tile of room all round.

Every step here is on the robot. None has been performed yet. The step names and the messages
quoted are read from the tuner code in `pedro/procedures/`, and the address from Pedro's
[Mecanum page](https://pedropathing.com/docs/pathing/tuning/drivetrain/mecanum), opened on
2026-10-05. The AutoTune page itself has not been seen, so its button names are not given.
::::

## The steps

### L100S010: open AutoTune

AutoTune is a web page the robot serves. It is not in the Driver Station's list of OpModes. It
starts its own OpMode, called `Pedro Tuning`, when a step needs the robot to move.

**Send.** Send the code to the robot, as in L050. Join the laptop to the robot's Wi-Fi. In a
browser, open `http://192.168.43.1:10158`.

The first time the browser talks to the robot, the operating system might ask for your
permission. Please give it.

**Check.** The page lists four tuners: `Mecanum Tuner`, `Tests`, `Pinpoint Tuner` and
`Foresight Tuner`.

### L100S020: find which way each motor turns

Put the robot on blocks, so its wheels spin freely.

**Robot.** Pick `Mecanum Tuner`. Type the four motor names as the Robot Configuration spells them:
`Front Left`, `Front Right`, `Back Left` and `Back Right`. Each motor then spins on its own, at
half power, beside a picture of which wheel it is. Say whether it spun, and whether it spun forward
or reversed.

**Change.** At the end, AutoTune shows Java that starts `public static MecanumConfig
drivetrainConfig`. Open `Constants`, in the `pedro` folder. Find the eight lines that set the names
and directions. Put the eight AutoTune shows in their place. Keep `manualBrakeMode` and
`powerThreshold`. AutoTune does not print them.

**Check.** The directions match what L050 found on blocks.

### L100S030: measure the pods

The Pinpoint needs four more numbers: which way each pod counts, and how far each one sits from the
middle of the robot. The x offset is how far sideways the forward pod is from the point the robot
turns about, and left counts up. The y offset is how far forward the strafe pod is, and forward
counts up. This robot's are about 1.06 in and 3.36 in.

Put the robot on the floor.

**Robot.** Pick `Pinpoint Tuner`. Keep the name `pinpoint` and the pod type it offers. Then do the
three pushes it asks for, stopping each one when you are done:

- Push the robot forward.
- Push the robot to the left.
- Spin the robot half a turn on the spot, counterclockwise.

**Change.** Replace `localizerConfig` in `Constants` with the `PinpointConfig` AutoTune shows.

**Check.** In `Tests`, pick `Localization Test` and drive with the gamepad. Driving forward raises
x, and strafing left raises y.

### L100S040: let the robot find its speed and its brakes

This one drives itself. Clear the floor first.

**Robot.** Pick `Foresight Tuner`. Keep the distances and the speed it offers: 48 in, 30 in/s and
36 in. It drives forward and left as fast as it can, coasts to a stop, turns back and forth, spins,
brakes, and steps 12 in to 24 in each way.

**Change.** Replace `foresightConfig` in `Constants` with the `ForesightConfig` AutoTune shows.
Keep the five lines it does not print, from `translationalConstraint` to `holdPointHeadingScaling`.

Three of its numbers are kept twice in `Constants`, for the lessons' own drivetrain. Copy each one
up into its own line:

- the number in `c.coast.set(...)` goes in `powerPerInchPerSecond`;
- `maxAchievableForwardVelocity` goes in `maxForwardInchesPerSecond`;
- `maxAchievableStrafeVelocity` goes in `maxStrafeInchesPerSecond`.

**Check.** Each of the three pairs holds the same number.

### L100S050: watch it drive a line

**Send.** Send the code again, so the new `Constants` is on the robot.

**Robot.** In `Tests`, pick `Line Test`, with the distance at 48.

**Check.** The robot drives 48 in and stops on the line.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad**, except in L100S030's check. AutoTune runs its own OpMode, and nothing here runs in
the simulator.

**If it didn't**, and the Mecanum Tuner stops with this, a motor other than the one named spun, or
none did:

```
The wrong motor spun. Check that your motors are plugged into the correct ports, and that they are configured correctly. Then, try again.
```

It is the message for an answer of No to "Did the front left motor spin?", read from
`MecanumTuner.java`. Swap two motor names and each of those motors is given the other's direction,
so both wheels turn backwards. L050's checks on blocks cannot tell that from a backwards wheel. This
one can.

**If it didn't**, and x and y swap over in the Localization Test, the two pods are in each other's
sockets. L115S010 is where that gets fixed, and no tuning works around it. This is from the old
`pinpoint` page, and has not been seen on a robot.

**If it didn't**, and the robot brakes late or never holds still after L100S040, check that the
five hold lines are still in `foresightConfig`. Read from the code, not seen.

**What you just did.** Every number in `Constants.java` either came off this robot or is waiting
to. AutoTune measures the ones that need the robot driving itself. L195 measures the two it does
not: ticks per inch and the turn radius.

**Where next.** [L110](l110.md) hands the wheels to the path follower, which drives with these
numbers.
