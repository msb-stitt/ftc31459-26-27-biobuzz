# 122 lesson: compare your localizer with the Pinpoint's

Your localizer from L113 runs beside the robot's own again. Now the robot's own reads the Pinpoint,
which L115 mounted and L117 tuned. Both are published so they can be graphed against each other.
Neither is the truth. A tape measure is.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L113 is done, so `mytry` holds `L113EncoderLocalizer`, and L117 is done, so the Pinpoint is on
  the robot and tuned.
- AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop, and a gamepad is plugged into the laptop.
- The robot on blocks, as in L050, the Driver Station, a tape measure, and about 3 m of clear floor
  for the last step.
::::

## the steps

### L122S010: copy the starter into mytry

**Copy.** In `lessons`, click `L122CompareLocalizersOpMode` once and copy it. Click `mytry` once
and paste. In the **Copy Class** box, click **OK**. If Android Studio asks whether to add the file
to Git, click **Add**.

**Check.**

- `mytry` holds `L122CompareLocalizersOpMode`, beside your `L113EncoderLocalizer`.

### L122S020: switch your copy on

**Change.** In `L122CompareLocalizersOpMode`, delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that
names this step.

**Check.**

- Under the step's comment, the next line reads
  `// 'public' so the robot can run this OpMode. 'public' allows access from any code.`

### L122S030: run your localizer beside the Pinpoint's

**Change.** In `L122CompareLocalizersOpMode`, find `shadowLocalizers()` and the comment that names
this step. Under it, type the same three lines as in L113S030:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-6

        // When on L122S030, see
        // https://coding.stitt.cc/ftc-lessons-java/guide/tasks/l122.html#l122s030
        // for what to do here.
        HardwareWheelSource wheelSource = new HardwareWheelSource(hardware);
        L113EncoderLocalizer driveWheelEncoders = new L113EncoderLocalizer(wheelSource);
        shadowLocalizers.add("driveWheelEncoders", driveWheelEncoders);
```

`HardwareWheelSource` turns red. Click it and press Option+Enter, as in L113.
`L113EncoderLocalizer` does not turn red. It is your own, from L113, and it sits in `mytry` too.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L122CompareLocalizersOpMode --pad"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020. Open the `Localizer` folder, then
`driveWheelEncoders` and `pinPoint` inside it.

**Check.**

- Push the left stick forward. `driveWheelEncoders/y_in` climbs with `pinPoint/y_in`, and the two
  stay within a tenth of an inch.

### L122S040: drive it, then measure it

**Robot.** Stop the simulator. Send the code, run `L122 Compare Localizers` from the Driver
Station, and watch it in AdvantageScope.

**Check.**

- Drive about 80 in straight ahead and stop. Both `y_in` numbers moved, and they do not quite agree.
- Measure how far the robot really went with the tape measure.

Then put the robot on the blocks and push the left stick forward. The wheels spin, and the robot
does not go anywhere. `driveWheelEncoders` walks off across the field. `pinPoint` stays put,
because its pods are off the floor too.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** `--args="mytry.L122CompareLocalizersOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.5"` drives forward.

**If it didn't**, and `driveWheelEncoders` is missing from the `Localizer` folder in L122S030, the
three lines are not in `shadowLocalizers()`. `LessonsTest.l122_bothLocalizersAreLogged` fails with
`the shadow localizer is logged:` and the list of names that were published.

**What you just did.** Your localizer believes the drive wheels, and a wheel that spins without
carrying the robot moves its estimate. The Pinpoint's pods only measure and never push, so they
slip less. That is the case for having them.
