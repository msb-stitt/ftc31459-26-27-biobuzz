## the steps

### L115S010: put one pod along the robot and one across it

The Pinpoint counts two wheels that nothing drives. One rolls along the length of the robot, and
that one is the **forward** pod. The other rolls across it, and that one is the **strafe** pod.

Both have to touch the floor the whole time, and neither may be able to lift. A pod that skips loses
count, and nothing after it can tell that it did.

**Mount.** Bolt the two pods on, one along the robot and one across it. Plug the forward pod into
the Pinpoint's **x** socket and the strafe pod into its **y** socket.

**Check.**

- Push the robot forward by hand. The forward pod's wheel turns.
- The strafe pod's wheel drags sideways.

### L115S020: plug it into its I2C port

The Pinpoint talks to the Control Hub over I2C. Port 0 already has the Control Hub's own IMU on it,
so the Pinpoint goes on another port. In L045S030 you named a device `pinpoint` on one of the other
I2C buses, and wrote down which. That is the name the code asks for:

```{code-block} java
:caption: pedro/Bot*.java
c.name.set("pinpoint");
```

**Mount.** Bolt the Pinpoint on with its sticker side, the side the ports are on, facing **up**.
Plug its cable into the Control Hub's I2C port with that bus's number.

**Robot.** Pick `L110 Follower` from the `Lessons` group and press INIT, but not PLAY. Connect
AdvantageScope to the robot as in L050, and drag `heading_deg` from `Localizer/pinPoint` into
**Left Axis**. Turn the robot a quarter turn by hand.

**Check.**

- `Localizer/pinPoint/heading_deg` changes by about 90 as the robot turns.

Only a Pinpoint that was found can tell the robot turned. Until now, INIT also warned that the
Pinpoint was missing, and now it does not.

### L115S030: hold the robot still while it inits

Pressing INIT builds the follower, and the follower builds the Pinpoint. The Pinpoint then resets
its own gyro, which takes about a quarter of a second. The robot has to sit still for it.

**Robot.** Press STOP. Put the robot on the floor, step back, safety glasses on! Pick
`L110 Follower` on the Driver Station and press INIT, but not PLAY yet. Watch it in AdvantageScope.

**Check.**

- Before you press PLAY, `Localizer/pinPoint/heading_deg` holds steady while the robot sits still.

**Drive.** Press PLAY and drive a slow square.

**Check.**

- The heading comes back to where it started.

::::{only} mentor
**If it didn't**, and a pod wheel does not turn at all in L115S010, it is not reaching the floor.
Most mounts have a slot for that.

**If it didn't**, and the Driver Station shows this at INIT, the name in the configuration is not
the name in the code:

```
Unable to find a hardware device with name "pinpoint" and type GoBildaPinpointDriver
```

A still robot cannot show whether the numbers are live; the square shows that.

**If it didn't**, and the heading crawls one way while the robot sits still, INIT happened while
the robot was moving. Press STOP and INIT again without touching it.

**If it didn't**, and forward and sideways come out swapped once L117 has tuned the pods, the two
pods are in each other's sockets. L115S010 is where that gets fixed, and no amount of tuning works
around it.

**Where the rest comes from.** The missing-device message is the one `HardwareMap.get` throws in
the SDK. The reset at INIT is `PinpointConfig`'s default `resetMode`, which `PinpointLocalizer`'s
constructor acts on. The still robot and the quarter second are from the goBILDA driver's own
javadoc.

**What you just did.** Every lesson from L117 on believes the Pinpoint. L132 puts your L113
localizer beside it and shows the drive encoders drifting away from it over a run. L131
measures the robot against it. A pod in the wrong socket makes all of that quietly wrong instead of
loudly broken.

The Pinpoint needs nothing from the drive motors to know where the robot is. Its two wheels are not
driven, so a mecanum roller cannot scrub them sideways and a slipping wheel cannot spin them.

**Where next.** [L117](l117.md) lets AutoTune tune the robot.
