# L140: make the sticks mean the field

:::{figure} ../_static/figures/fig-l140-spin-and-slide.png
:name: fig-l140-spin-and-slide

AdvantageScope's 2D Field with the robot right of where it started and turned partway round, with the list showing `arcade/forward` at 0.160, `arcade/turn` at 0.490, and `pinPoint/x_in` at 107.911, `y_in` at 20.901 and `heading_deg` at 234.
:::

Push the stick away from you and the robot goes away from you. It does that whichever way the robot
happens to be facing, so a driver never has to think about which end of the robot is the front.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L130 is done.
- AdvantageScope is installed on the same laptop, and a gamepad is plugged into the laptop.
- The robot, the Driver Station, and about 2 m by 2 m of clear floor for the last step.

Step L140S050 is written from the code and the old `l11` page. It has not been performed on a robot
yet.

**Where the driver stands.** "Away from the driver" is the field's +x, as in the old `l11`. The
robot starts at heading 90, facing +y, so at the start a push away from the driver slides it to its
right. Which wall the drivers stand at this season has not been checked against the game manual.
::::

## The steps

### L140S010: copy the starter into mytry

**Copy.** In `lessons`, click `L140FieldRelativeOpMode` once and copy it. Click `mytry` once and
paste. In the **Copy Class** box, click **OK**. If Android Studio asks whether to add the file to
Git, click **Add**.

**Check.** `mytry` holds `L140FieldRelativeOpMode`, and the new file is open.

### L140S020: switch your copy on

**Change.** Delete the line `@Disabled`, under the comment that names this step.

**Check.** Under the step's comment, the next line reads
`// public: the robot only runs an OpMode that is public.`

### L140S030: drive and turn at once, the L110 way

The starter drives the way L110 did. Forward on the stick is forward for the robot.

**Simulator.** In the Terminal, run:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:simRun --args="mytry.L140FieldRelativeOpMode --pad"
```

**AdvantageScope.** Connect to the simulator as in L020, click **2D Field**, and open the
`Localizer` folder, then `pinPoint`.

**Check.** Push the left stick a little forward and the right stick left, and hold both. The robot
drives in a small circle, and `x_in` and `y_in` stay close to where they started. It goes where its
nose points, and its nose keeps turning.

### L140S040: turn the stick backwards through the heading

:::{figure} ../_static/figures/fig-field-relative.png
:name: fig-field-relative

The field from above with the driver at the bottom, the robot turned a quarter turn, the stick's direction as an arrow on the field, and the same arrow drawn again on the robot showing what forward and sideways it turns into.
:::

From now on, `forward` on the stick means away from the driver, along the field's x. `strafe` means
to the driver's left, along the field's y. On AdvantageScope's 2D Field, +x runs to the right and
+y runs up.

The wheels only know the robot's own forward and sideways. L120 turned a step on the robot into a
step on the field, through the heading. This turns the other way, from the field to the robot, so
the signs on `sin` swap.

**Change.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. In `loop()`, find the comment that
names this step. Delete the line `drivetrain.sticks(forward, strafe, turn);` under it, and type:

```{code-block} java
:emphasize-lines: 4-7

        // When on L140S040, see
        // https://msb-stitt.github.io/ftc31459-26-27-biobuzz/guide/tasks/l140.html#l140s040
        // for what to do here.
        double heading = follower.pose().heading();
        double robotForward = forward * Math.cos(heading) + strafe * Math.sin(heading);
        double robotStrafe = -forward * Math.sin(heading) + strafe * Math.cos(heading);
        drivetrain.sticks(robotForward, robotStrafe, turn);
```

The follower knows which way the robot faces. The drivetrain does not. So the OpMode asks the
follower, and does the turn itself.

**Simulator.** Run the same command again, and connect AdvantageScope.

**Check.** Hold the same two sticks. The robot spins, and at the same time it travels to the right
on the 2D Field, along +x. `x_in` climbs while `heading_deg` goes round and round.

### L140S050: drive it facing the wrong way

**Send.** Press Ctrl+C in the Terminal to stop the simulator. Send the code to the robot, as in
L050. On the Driver Station, pick `L140 Field Relative` from the `Lessons` group. Stand off the
robot's left side, so that away from you is the field's +x. Press INIT, then PLAY.

**Check.** Push the right stick until the robot's nose points at you. Then push the left stick away
from you. The robot drives away from you while it faces you. Turn it a quarter turn and try again,
and the stick still means the same way on the field.

::::{only} mentor
**No gamepad.** `--args="mytry.L140FieldRelativeOpMode --left_stick_y=-0.4 --right_stick_x=-0.7"`
holds both sticks, for the circle in L140S030 and the picture at the top. Before L140S040, the run
was watched at `x_in` 70.812 and `y_in` 7.230 after about 8 s.

After L140S040, the robot drifts toward +y as it goes: one run went from x 72 and y 10.5 to x 179
and y 43.5, about 17 degrees off a straight line. Why has not been worked out. The simulator has no
walls, so a run left going drives off the field.

**If it didn't**, and `LessonsTest.l140_fieldRelativeIgnoresWhichWayTheRobotFaces` fails with
`same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<1.0>`, the line still reads
`drivetrain.sticks(forward, strafe, turn);`.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with `expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`, the turn runs the wrong way.
The minus sign is missing from `-forward`, or both signs on `sin` are the other way round.

**If it didn't**, and it fails with
`same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<-0.3338674754196965>`, the
heading went in as degrees. `Math.cos` wants radians.

**What you just did.** Field relative is one turn of two numbers and nothing else. The same
`sticks`, the same mix, the same scaling. All that changed is which pair of numbers goes in.

It also depends on the follower knowing which way the robot faces. If the heading drifts 20
degrees, the sticks are 20 degrees crooked. This is the first lesson where a mistake somewhere
else in the robot shows up in the driver's hands.

**Where next.** [L150](l150.md) puts the robot's own directions back on a bumper, so a driver can
have both.
::::
