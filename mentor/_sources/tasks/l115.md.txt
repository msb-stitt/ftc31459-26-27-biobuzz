# L115: mount the Pinpoint and plug it in

:::{figure} ../_static/figures/fig-pinpoint-mounting.png
:name: fig-pinpoint-mounting

The Pinpoint on the robot from above, with the forward pod and the strafe pod drawn at right angles to each other, each labelled with the socket it plugs into, and the sticker side marked facing up.
:::

The Pinpoint is bolted on, its two pods are in the right sockets, and the Control Hub finds it when
you press INIT. The robot does not yet know how far the pods sit from its middle. L100 measures
that.

::::{only} mentor
**Before you start.**

- L110 is done, so the follower holds the drivetrain. The follower is what builds the Pinpoint.
- The robot, the two odometry pods, and whatever driver the robot's own screws take.
- The I2C cable the Pinpoint came with, and the Driver Station.

Every step here is on the robot. None has been performed yet. The three rules in L115S010 and
L115S020 are Pedro's, from
[its Pinpoint page](https://pedropathing.com/docs/pathing/tuning/localization/pinpoint), read on
2026-09-28 and again on 2026-10-05. No Pinpoint was wired each wrong way to see what each mistake
looks like.
::::

## The steps

### L115S010: put one pod along the robot and one across it

The Pinpoint counts two wheels that nothing drives. One rolls along the length of the robot, and
that one is the **forward** pod. The other rolls across it, and that one is the **strafe** pod.

Both have to touch the floor the whole time, and neither may be able to lift. A pod that skips loses
count, and nothing after it can tell that it did.

**Mount.** Bolt the two pods on, one along the robot and one across it. Plug the forward pod into
the Pinpoint's **x** socket and the strafe pod into its **y** socket.

**Check.**

- Push the robot forward by hand. The forward pod's wheel turns.
- The strafe pod's wheel drags sideways.

### L115S020: plug it into an I2C port that is not port 0

The Pinpoint talks to the Control Hub over I2C. Port 0 already has the Control Hub's own IMU on it,
so the Pinpoint goes on another port.

**Mount.** Bolt the Pinpoint on with its sticker side, the side the ports are on, facing **up**.
Plug its cable into I2C port 1, 2 or 3 on the Control Hub.

**Configure.** On the Driver Station, open the robot's configuration. Add the Pinpoint on that
port, and name it `pinpoint`, spelled exactly that way. That is the name the code asks for:

```java
c.name.set("pinpoint");
```

**Check.**

- Pick `L110 Follower` from the `Lessons` group and press INIT.
- INIT finishes with no error.

INIT looks for the Pinpoint by its name, so if INIT finishes, it was found.

### L115S030: hold the robot still while it inits

Pressing INIT builds the follower, and the follower builds the Pinpoint. The Pinpoint then resets
its own gyro, which takes about a quarter of a second. The robot has to sit still for it.

**Robot.** Put the robot on the floor, step back, safety glasses on! Pick `L110 Follower` on the
Driver Station and press INIT, but not PLAY yet. Watch it in AdvantageScope.

**Check.**

- Before you press PLAY, `Localizer/pinPoint/heading_deg` holds steady while the robot sits still.

**Drive.** Press PLAY and drive a slow square.

**Check.**

- The heading comes back to where it started.

::::{only} mentor
**If it didn't**, and a pod wheel does not turn at all in L115S010, it is not reaching the floor.
Most mounts have a slot for that.

**If it didn't**, and the Driver Station shows this at INIT, the name in the configuration is not
the name in the code:

```
Unable to find a hardware device with name "pinpoint" and type GoBildaPinpointDriver
```

**Seen on 2026-10-06**, on blocks, not on the floor: after INIT and before PLAY, AdvantageScope
read `heading_deg` 90.000, `x_in` 72.000 and `y_in` 10.500, and the same 36 s later. A still robot
cannot show whether the numbers are live; the square shows that, and it has not been driven.

**If it didn't**, and the heading crawls one way while the robot sits still, INIT happened while
the robot was moving. Press STOP and INIT again without touching it.

**If it didn't**, and forward and sideways come out swapped once L100 has tuned the pods, the two
pods are in each other's sockets. L115S010 is where that gets fixed, and no amount of tuning works
around it.

**Where the rest comes from.** The missing-device message is the one `HardwareMap.get` throws in
the SDK. The reset at INIT is `PinpointConfig`'s default `resetMode`, which `PinpointLocalizer`'s
constructor acts on. The still robot and the quarter second are from the goBILDA driver's own
javadoc. These were read from the code, not seen on a robot.

**What you just did.** Every lesson from L120 on believes the Pinpoint. L120 puts a second way of
tracking the robot beside it and shows the drive encoders drifting away from it over a run. L195
measures the robot against it. A pod in the wrong socket makes all of that quietly wrong instead of
loudly broken.

The Pinpoint needs nothing from the drive motors to know where the robot is. Its two wheels are not
driven, so a mecanum roller cannot scrub them sideways and a slipping wheel cannot spin them.

**Where next.** [L120](l120.md) compares the Pinpoint with the drive encoders and logs both.
::::
